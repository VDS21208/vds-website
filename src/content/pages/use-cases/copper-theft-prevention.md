## What copper theft looks like in 2026

Scrap copper sits near $4 per pound. A single substation hit yields hundreds of pounds of grounding wire, transformer windings, and bus bar. The street value of that haul is $1,500 to $4,000. The replacement and downtime cost to the utility is $80,000 to $300,000 — and that's before regulatory penalties for an outage.

The crime pattern is consistent. Suspects scout during daylight, return between 1am and 4am, cut through fencing or use a ladder over it, and work fast. Construction sites lose copper feeder wire from generators and panels. Telecom huts lose grounding bars. Oil and gas pads lose copper bonding straps. Abandoned buildings get stripped of wire and pipe in a single night.

Static surveillance cameras record the theft. They do not prevent it. The suspects have masks and a stolen vehicle. By the time the footage is reviewed, the copper is at a scrap yard 80 miles away.

## How VDS prevents copper theft

Sky Guard MSU is built for this threat. Thermal imaging detects body heat through brush, fence cover, and low light — the visual camera misses the suspect, the thermal camera does not. Radar detects movement at the property line before a perimeter breach, giving the SOC 30 to 90 seconds of lead time.

AI VisionStream classifies the target. Person at the fence line at 2am, on foot, carrying tools — that is not a deer or a security false alarm. The rule fires, the alert hits a US SOC operator's screen, and the operator initiates live audio talk-down through the unit's speaker: "You are on private utility property. Law enforcement has been dispatched. Leave the area now."

In our case data, audio talk-down ends the incident before any wire is touched in the majority of cases. When it doesn't, the operator dispatches local police with a live video feed and exact GPS coordinates.

## What cargo theft looks like in 2026

CargoNet reported a sustained surge in strategic cargo theft through 2023 and 2024 — fictitious pickups, identity theft of legitimate carriers, double-brokered loads, and yard infiltrations. The top targets remain consistent: electronics, food and beverage, household goods, and apparel. A single full trailer can carry $100,000 to $500,000 in product. Some loads exceed $1 million.

The crime moves fast. A tractor with cloned DOT credentials backs into a dock, drops the legitimate trailer, hooks up the target, and pulls out — in under 15 minutes. Or a perimeter breach at 3am, a stolen yard tractor, and a high-value trailer is gone before the dispatcher notices. Recovery rates are below 25%. Insurance covers some of it; the customer relationship damage covers none of it.

Static cameras at the gate see the truck. They do not see that the trailer hookup at dock 14 at 2:47am was unauthorized. They do not alert anyone in time to stop it.

## How VDS prevents cargo theft

VDS treats the yard as a system. Boundary Guard Pro runs the fence-line — every approach to the perimeter triggers an alert before the breach. Swift Deploy units at every vehicle entrance run LPR with 95%+ accuracy in under 3 seconds, logging every tractor and trailer plate against your authorized list.

AI VisionStream rules catch the patterns static cameras miss. Trailer-hookup detection during after-hours windows fires when a tractor docks without a corresponding dispatch record. Loitering rules alert on a vehicle parked at the property line for more than 4 minutes. The SOC operator pulls live video, verifies in seconds, and either talks the driver down through the on-unit speaker or dispatches law enforcement with plate, GPS, and direction of travel.

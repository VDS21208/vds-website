## What Is NDAA Section 889?

<p>The National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA) Section 889 was enacted as part of the 2019 National Defense Authorization Act to address national security concerns regarding telecommunications and video surveillance equipment. Section 889 prohibits federal agencies from procuring or using equipment and services from companies that utilize covered telecommunications or video surveillance equipment from five specific manufacturers: Huawei Technologies, ZTE Corporation, Hytera Communications, Hangzhou Hikvision, and Dahua Technology.</p>

<p>The law contains two critical parts: Part A prevents federal agencies from directly purchasing equipment from these restricted manufacturers, while Part B extends the prohibition to federal contractors, preventing them from using this equipment anywhere in their operations. Part B became effective August 13, 2020, and applies to any organization seeking federal contracts or grants.</p>

<p>Beyond federal procurement, NDAA Section 889 compliance requirements have extended to state and local government agencies receiving federal funding, creating widespread impact across public sector surveillance and security programs. Many regulated industries now require NDAA compliance as a standard procurement criterion.</p>



## Who NDAA Section 889 Affects

<p>NDAA Section 889 compliance requirements extend across multiple sectors and procurement programs:</p>

<ul>

<li><strong>Federal Agencies</strong> — All executive departments including DOD, DHS, GSA, and their procurement programs must verify contractor compliance before award</li>

<li><strong>State and Local Government</strong> — Agencies receiving federal funding through grants, cooperative agreements, or federal program participation must demonstrate compliance</li>

<li><strong>Federal Contractors and Subcontractors</strong> — Any organization bidding on federal contracts must certify that they do not use restricted equipment in their operations</li>

<li><strong>Critical Infrastructure Operators</strong> — Power, water, transportation, and communication networks often require NDAA compliance for security system procurements</li>

<li><strong>Public Safety Agencies</strong> — Law enforcement, emergency management, and public safety organizations receiving federal grants must meet compliance standards</li>

<li><strong>Regulated Procurement Programs</strong> — GSA schedule, cooperative purchasing agreements, and state contract vehicles increasingly require NDAA compliance certification</li>

</ul>



## Risks of Non-Compliance

<p>Organizations that fail to meet NDAA Section 889 requirements face significant procurement and operational consequences. Contract disqualification represents the most immediate risk — procurement teams cannot award contracts to vendors who cannot certify compliance, regardless of technical capabilities or pricing advantages.</p>

<p>Federal contractors face suspension or debarment from all federal contracting opportunities if found using restricted equipment. The Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) requires contractors to remove any covered equipment from their systems, creating costly operational disruption and potential security gaps during transition periods.</p>

<p>Criminal penalties apply to organizations that provide false certifications regarding their compliance status. Procurement audits increasingly require component-level documentation to verify compliance claims, making inadequate due diligence a significant legal and business risk.</p>

<p>Beyond regulatory consequences, non-compliant security systems may require complete replacement if discovered during contract performance, creating budget overruns and coverage gaps that expose facilities to security incidents during system transitions.</p>

## NDAA Compliance FAQ



### What is NDAA Section 889?

<p>NDAA Section 889 is federal legislation that prohibits government agencies and federal contractors from using telecommunications and video surveillance equipment from five specific manufacturers: Huawei, ZTE, Hikvision, Dahua, and Hytera. The law applies to federal agencies directly and extends to contractors, subcontractors, and organizations receiving federal funding.</p>



### Which surveillance camera brands are banned under NDAA Section 889?

<p>Section 889 specifically restricts equipment from Hangzhou Hikvision Digital Technology and Dahua Technology Company, along with telecommunications equipment from Huawei Technologies, ZTE Corporation, and Hytera Communications Corporation. The restriction applies to equipment manufactured by these companies or containing their components.</p>



### Does NDAA compliance apply to state and local government?

<p>Yes, NDAA Section 889 applies to state and local government agencies that receive federal funding through grants, cooperative agreements, or federal program participation. Many state procurement programs now require NDAA compliance regardless of federal funding to maintain consistent security standards across government operations.</p>



### How do I verify that security equipment is NDAA compliant?

<p>Request written compliance attestation letters from manufacturers documenting that their hardware and software contain no components from restricted manufacturers. Verify that suppliers can provide component-level documentation and maintain compliant supply chains. Work directly with manufacturers rather than resellers to ensure compliance accountability.</p>



### Can VDS provide written NDAA compliance documentation for procurement?

<p>Yes, VDS provides comprehensive NDAA Section 889 compliance documentation including signed attestation letters, component-level supply chain documentation, and procurement support for federal, state, and cooperative purchasing programs.

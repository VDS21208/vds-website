## Why a plant page

Most security companies do not have a plant. They have a warehouse, a procurement team, and a label-maker. When something fails, the answer is a ticket to a distributor and a four-week lead time on a part that came from someone else's container.

We are not that. This page exists because the difference matters — for compliance, for service, and for the customers who ask, fairly, "where does this come from?"

## What happens at the Pikesville facility

The plant covers chassis integration, electrical assembly, mast and solar mounting, programming and firmware loading, and final QC. Every unit that ships to a customer was on the floor here first.

**Chassis and frame integration.** Trailer chassis arrive from US suppliers. VDS technicians complete the structural integration — mast mounts, equipment bay framing, weatherproofing, and finish work.

**Solar and power systems.** Solar panels are sourced from named NDAA-compliant vendors. Battery banks, charge controllers, and DC distribution are wired and tested on-bench before installation in the unit. Each system is load-tested for the duty cycle it will see in the field.

**Camera and sensor integration.** Cameras (from named NDAA-compliant manufacturers — full list available on request) are mounted, aligned, and weather-sealed. Thermal sensors, radar where specified, and audio deterrents are integrated and bench-tested.

**Programming and platform provisioning.** Each unit is provisioned onto AI VisionStream at the plant. Site templates, alarm zones, and SOC rule sets are pre-loaded. Cellular modems are activated and connectivity verified before shipment.

**Quality control and inspection.** Every unit passes a multi-point inspection: structural, electrical, optical alignment, network handshake to AWS, SOC visibility test, and 72-hour burn-in for solar-power-cycle stability. Inspection records are tied to the unit serial number and retained for the life of the asset.

## In-house vs. sourced

We are honest about what we make and what we buy. The line matters for traceability.

**Done at VDS:** chassis integration, mast assemblies, electrical and DC systems, weatherproofing, programming and firmware, camera mounting and alignment, platform provisioning, QC and burn-in, packaging and shipping.

**Sourced from named vendors:** raw trailer chassis (US suppliers), solar panels (NDAA-compliant US/allied vendors), cameras (named NDAA-compliant manufacturers), batteries (US/allied), cellular modems (US carriers, NDAA-compliant hardware). Vendor list is documented per unit and available to compliance-sensitive customers under NDA.

## Why this matters

**NDAA Section 889 traceability.** Compliance is not a sticker. It is a bill of materials you can defend in front of a federal contracting officer. Because we control assembly, we control the BOM — and we can produce documentation per unit, per serial number, on request.

**Faster service parts.** A camera or power module that fails in Texas does not wait on a container from overseas. Replacements ship from Pikesville, typically same-day or next-day for service-contracted accounts. The same techs who built the unit support it in the field.

**US jobs.** The plant employs technicians, electricians, programmers, and QC inspectors locally. Every unit that ships funds those roles. We are not abstract about this — manufacturing in Maryland is a deliberate choice, and it costs more than offshore assembly. The trade is worth it.

**Iteration speed.** Field feedback loops back into the next production run. When the SOC notices a pattern — a camera angle that creates false alarms, a power profile that fails in shoulder-season cold — engineering changes the next build. Resellers cannot do this. Manufacturers can.

## Visiting the plant

Customers and prospects are welcome to tour the facility. We schedule tours weekly. You will see the floor, meet the team, and watch a unit go through QC. Federal and utility customers conducting compliance audits get a structured walkthrough with documentation.

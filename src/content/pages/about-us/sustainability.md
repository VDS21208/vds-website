A typical jobsite security alternative to a VDS mobile unit is one of two things: a trenched grid hookup with a permanent meter, or a diesel light tower with a 24/7 generator. Both have measurable environmental and economic costs that VDS units avoid by design. This page documents the math.

## The energy baseline

A diesel light tower running surveillance and lighting at a typical construction perimeter consumes approximately 0.6 gallons of diesel per hour. At 24-hour operation, that is 14.4 gallons per day, or roughly 5,250 gallons per year per tower. At current commercial diesel pricing, that is between $20,000 and $25,000 per year per tower in fuel cost alone, plus weekly fuel-delivery truck rolls.

A VDS Sky Guard MSU consumes approximately 11.5 kWh per day under typical 24/7 operation including IR illumination, cellular uplink, edge AI inference, and onboard recording. That energy is supplied by a 1.2 kW solar array with a 25 kWh lithium battery bank sized for 7 days of zero-sun autonomy.

**Fuel consumed per VDS unit per year: zero gallons of diesel.**

## The truck-roll comparison

A diesel tower needs a fuel delivery truck once every 9 to 12 days. Over a 12-month deployment, that is roughly 35 truck rolls per unit, each one with associated emissions and road wear.

A VDS unit needs a service visit on a quarterly maintenance schedule — 4 truck rolls per year for routine inspection, with most service calls handled remotely via the VMS without dispatch. Unplanned site visits average 0.8 per unit per year across the deployed fleet.

**Total truck rolls per VDS unit per year: approximately 5. Reduction versus diesel baseline: 86%.**

## Battery and component lifecycle

The lithium iron phosphate (LiFePO4) battery banks in current VDS units are rated for 6,000+ deep-discharge cycles at 80% depth of discharge. In a typical deployment, that corresponds to a service life of approximately 10–12 years before significant capacity degradation. End-of-life batteries are recycled through partnered LiFePO4 recyclers — not landfilled.

Solar panels are monocrystalline silicon with a 25-year manufacturer warranty and an expected service life of 30 years. Component reuse across unit refurbishment is the standard practice: we have units in field service with panels and battery cells that have been redeployed across three different chassis.

**Comparison:** A grid-tied site requires permanent trenching, conduit, transformers, and metered service. Removing that infrastructure at project end is rare — it usually stays in the ground. Solar units leave no trenching behind.

## Emissions math, on the record

Per unit, per year, against a diesel-tower baseline:

- **Approximately 4,200 kWh** of grid-equivalent or fuel-equivalent energy not consumed
- **Approximately 32 metric tons** of CO2 equivalent not emitted (diesel combustion at 22.4 lbs CO2 per gallon)
- **Approximately 30 fewer truck rolls**, with associated diesel emissions and tire/brake particulate

Across 500+ deployments, the cumulative annual figure is on the order of 16,000 metric tons of CO2e avoided versus the diesel baseline. We do not market against the grid baseline because the comparison is highly site-specific.

## What we do not claim

We do not claim carbon neutrality. We do not buy offsets to pad a marketing number. We do not run a corporate sustainability report with stock photography. The supply chain for solar panels, lithium batteries, and aluminum chassis has its own environmental cost — we are honest about that.

What we claim is a unit-economics-driven product design that happens to produce significantly better environmental outcomes than the alternatives. The customer's accounting team sees this on the operating cost line. The site's environmental compliance team sees it on the air quality and noise reports.

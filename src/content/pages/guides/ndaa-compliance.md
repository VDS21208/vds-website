## Free excerpt: the three procurement traps

**Trap 1: "NDAA-compliant" without Section 889 specificity.** Vendors throw "NDAA-compliant" on a spec sheet referring to NDAA 2018 or some other provision. Section 889 is the prohibition that matters for surveillance. Ask for written Part B certification with the manufacturer's name and country of origin per component.

**Trap 2: OEM rebrands.** Hikvision and Dahua chips and firmware appear inside dozens of US-labeled brands. The 2021 FCC Covered List and subsequent enforcement actions have flagged rebadged units sold under "American" brand names. Request the FCC FRN and the camera's actual ODM (original design manufacturer).

**Trap 3: Telecom backhaul.** Section 889 covers the radios and cellular modems too, not just cameras. A compliant camera on a Huawei-chipset modem still fails. Verify the cellular module manufacturer (Quectel, Telit, Cradlepoint, Sierra Wireless — confirm specific SKU).

## Free excerpt: state and utility flow-down

Section 889 is federal by statute, but its effect is broader. Three patterns we see daily:

1. **Federal grant pass-through.** State DOT, FEMA, BJA grants, and FAA airport improvement funds all carry Section 889 flow-down. Use the money, follow the rule.
2. **Utility procurement.** NERC-regulated utilities increasingly include Section 889 language in physical security procurement, particularly for substations and control centers covered under CIP-014.
3. **State adoption.** Texas, Florida, Georgia, Vermont, Louisiana, and South Dakota have enacted Section 889-aligned procurement bans. More on the way.

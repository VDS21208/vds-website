"Remote video monitoring" gets used by three different categories of provider to describe three different services. The guide separates them, explains what changes for buyers in each, and gives you the questions to tell them apart.

## What is inside

**Part 1: What remote video monitoring actually is** (pages 2-7)

Three operating models in the market today:

- **Event-driven monitoring** — cameras run AI analytics on-device or in cloud; only flagged events go to a human operator. This is the modern, scalable model. VDS operates here.
- **Continuous passive monitoring** — operators watch a video wall during defined hours. Expensive per camera; limited by human attention. Still used in casinos and some critical infrastructure.
- **Periodic patrol monitoring** — an operator cycles through camera feeds on a schedule, looking for anomalies. Cheap, low effectiveness, rarely sold under "AI" branding.

The guide explains where each fits and where each fails.

**Part 2: Inside a modern SOC** (pages 8-14)

- Operator workflow per event: receive alert, validate (visual + analytic), classify, act
- The action ladder: ignore, log, audio talk-down, customer notify, law-enforcement dispatch
- Audio talk-down — when it works (deterrence on opportunistic trespass) and when it does not (organized theft, vehicle pursuit)
- Law-enforcement dispatch authority and verified-response policies by state
- Onshore vs. offshore staffing — why this matters for response time and legal exposure

**Part 3: False-alarm reduction math** (pages 15-19)

The industry estimate is that 94-98% of traditional camera-triggered alerts are false positives — animals, wind-blown debris, shadows, headlights. Police response to false alarms is the single largest tax on residential and commercial monitoring.

This section walks through:

- How AI primary filtering reduces the false-positive rate at the camera
- How human secondary validation reduces it further
- The 97% false-alarm reduction figure VDS publishes, with the methodology behind it
- Why this directly drives verified-response credibility with local PDs

**Part 4: Response time** (pages 20-23)

What it means, how to measure it, what is realistic:

- Alert generation latency (camera to cloud to operator queue)
- Operator handle time (sub-60 seconds at VDS, industry varies 90s-5min)
- Escalation latency (operator to customer contact, operator to PD)
- End-to-end response: incident-on-camera to PD-on-scene, with caveats

Includes a worked example: a vehicle entering a logistics yard at 02:14, alarmed via virtual tripwire, validated, audio talk-down issued, PD dispatch, on-scene timestamps.

**Part 5: ROI model** (pages 24-28)

A copy-paste 3-year ROI template covering:

- Avoided incident cost (theft, vandalism, slip-and-fall, equipment loss)
- Insurance premium impact (where applicable)
- Guard labor reduction or replacement
- Productivity loss from false alarms eliminated
- Subscription cost vs. avoided cost over 36 months

The model accepts your real loss data; the worked example uses construction-site theft averages from CRIMECON and IICRC.

## Why this comparison matters

Type "mobile surveillance" into Google and the first page returns vendors selling completely different products. A construction GC and a sheriff's deputy both use the words "mobile surveillance" to describe the thing they need. They need different things.

This page exists to route you to the right category before you spend a quarter chasing the wrong vendor.

## The two markets

| Dimension | Fixed-Site Mobile Surveillance | Fleet / In-Car / Body-Worn Video |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Solar-autonomous surveillance trailers and pole-mounted units that deploy to a site for weeks or months | Video cameras mounted in vehicles or worn by personnel that record continuously while the asset is in motion |
| Where it lives | At a site — construction yard, lay-down area, utility substation, retail parking, remote property | Inside or on a vehicle or person — patrol car, transit bus, delivery truck, officer's chest |
| Mobility model | Redeployable between sites; static while on-site | Always moving with the vehicle or person |
| Power model | Solar autonomous, optional grid | Vehicle 12V or worn battery |
| Primary buyer | Construction, utility, retail, municipal, energy, federal sites | Law enforcement, transit, public works fleets, delivery, trucking |
| Primary use case | Theft deterrence, perimeter monitoring, post-incident evidence, remote site oversight | Officer accountability, fleet incident review, body-worn evidence chain, dashcam claims defense |
| Storage and chain of custody | Cloud-first, immutable audit log, RBAC, MFA | DEMS-style chain of custody, court-admissible export, prosecutor handoff |
| 24/7 monitoring | Often — VDS SOC runs this | Rarely — assets are mobile and operator-led |
| AI focus | LPR, person/vehicle/intrusion at perimeter | Driver behavior, collision detection, body-cam event flags |
| Procurement model | Rental or purchase per site / per unit | Per-vehicle or per-officer license, often multi-year contract |

## Leading vendors in fixed-site mobile surveillance

If you got here looking for solar-autonomous surveillance trailers, the shortlist is on /compare/top-mobile-surveillance-companies. The vendors are LVT, ECAM, Flock Safety, WCCTV, Pro-Vigil, Verkada, Spot AI, Deploy Surveillance, GoView, and VDS.

## How to tell which market you need

Answer these three questions:

1. **Does the camera need to move continuously with a vehicle or person?**
   - Yes → fleet / in-car / body-worn. Talk to Axon, Apollo, Safety Vision, AngelTrax, Digital Ally, Utility, or TSI Video.
   - No → fixed-site mobile surveillance. Talk to VDS.

2. **Will the camera sit at a fixed location for weeks or months, then redeploy to a new site?**
   - Yes → fixed-site mobile surveillance. This is VDS.
   - No → not VDS.

3. **Is your primary use case officer accountability, court-admissible body-cam evidence, or fleet driver behavior?**
   - Yes → fleet / in-car / body-worn.
   - No, your use case is theft deterrence, perimeter monitoring, and remote site oversight → fixed-site mobile surveillance.

## Where the two markets sometimes overlap

A utility operator might use VDS at a substation perimeter AND Apollo Video on their service trucks. A municipality might run Flock Safety ALPR poles in city neighborhoods AND Axon body cameras on the patrol force. The two markets serve adjacent buyers and sometimes the same buyer for different jobs.

The key is that the procurement, RFP language, evaluation criteria, and vendor lists are different. Buying body-worn cameras from a mobile surveillance trailer vendor is a category error. Buying mobile surveillance trailers from a body-cam vendor is also a category error.

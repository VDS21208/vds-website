## Why this comparison matters

VDS hardware is available two ways: monthly rental (the default and the most common) or outright purchase (less common, available for portfolios at a certain scale). The choice is a financial decision, not a product decision — the trailer, VMS, and SOC are identical either way. This page lays out when each shape is correct.

## The feature matrix

| Dimension | Monthly Rental | Outright Purchase |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly cost | $1,500-$2,200 all-in | $0 after purchase |
| Upfront cost | None | $45,000-$78,000 per unit |
| Hardware refresh | Included inside term | Customer responsibility after 5 years |
| Repair / replacement | Vendor-side (48-hour SLA) | Vendor-side under support contract |
| SOC included | Yes | Separate monthly per unit (~$800-$1,200) |
| VMS subscription | Yes | Separate monthly per unit (~$200-$400) |
| Cellular data | Yes | Customer pays carrier directly |
| Capex vs. opex | Opex | Capex (depreciation) |
| Procurement complexity | Low | Higher (PO, asset tagging) |
| Tax treatment | Deductible operating expense | Depreciable asset (5-7 yr) |
| Site portability | Easier (return at end) | Customer owns logistics |
| Best for | Projects 3-36 months, multi-site portfolios under 20 units | Stable 5+ year deployments, federal projects, fleets 20+ units |
| Break-even vs. rental | N/A | ~30 months at $2,200/mo |
| Termination flexibility | 30-90 day notice after term | Sell or store unit |

## When to rent

**Project-based work.** Construction projects, event security, seasonal coverage, temporary lots — rental is the right answer because you're not committing to a permanent asset for a temporary need.

**Multi-site portfolio under 20 units.** The administrative cost of capitalizing, asset-tagging, and tracking depreciation on 5-15 units rarely pays back vs. just renting. Rental keeps it on one MSA and one monthly invoice.

**Anyone who values opex over capex.** Site general conditions and security monthlies absorb rental easily. Capital budgets often don't.

**Anyone who wants hardware refresh included.** Our rental terms include hardware refresh — when we ship a new platform generation, your fleet rotates to it inside the rental term at no incremental cost. Purchase customers own a hardware vintage and decide when to upgrade.

**Anyone who doesn't want to own a depreciating asset.** Five years from now, you don't have to figure out the salvage value or storage of a rented unit.

## When to purchase

**Federal contracts with capitalization mandates.** Some federal programs (FEMA, DOE, certain DoD installations) require capital ownership of deployed infrastructure for compliance or accounting reasons. Purchase fits that model.

**Stable deployments expected to run 5+ years on the same site.** If you know with certainty a single unit will sit at the same site for 5+ years, the rental math is roughly $132,000 over 60 months vs. a purchase price plus support contract. The break-even math favors purchase at roughly month 30 of continuous deployment.

**Fleets of 20+ units in long-term programs.** At scale, customers with internal asset management teams sometimes prefer to own the fleet, capitalize it on the balance sheet, and contract VDS only for SOC + VMS + support.

**Specific tax positions.** Section 179 expensing or bonus depreciation may make a purchase year-one accounting attractive for some buyers. Talk to your tax advisor.

## Break-even math

For a single Sky Guard MSU at $2,200/mo rental vs. a $68,000 purchase + $1,000/mo combined SOC + VMS + support:

- **Rental cost at month 30:** $2,200 × 30 = $66,000
- **Purchase cost at month 30:** $68,000 + ($1,000 × 30) = $98,000
- **Rental wins through month 30, breakeven near month 32**, purchase pulls ahead at month 36+ assuming the hardware doesn't need replacement.

The big asterisk: hardware replacement cycle. Mobile surveillance hardware in a harsh outdoor environment has a 5-7 year useful life. At year 5 of ownership, you're spending $40,000-$60,000 to refresh. Rental customers get that refresh inside the term at no incremental cost. Adjust the math accordingly for any deployment beyond 60 months.

## Hybrid model — own some, rent some

Some larger customers run a hybrid fleet: purchase units for known-permanent sites, rent units for project-based or surge coverage. This works particularly well for utilities, municipalities, and large GCs running concurrent permanent and project deployments.

## How to think about it

Three questions:

1. **How long will this unit sit at one site?** Under 36 months: rent. 36-60 months: it's a coin flip, slightly favors rent. Over 60 months with stable site: consider purchase.

2. **How does your finance team prefer to book it?** If opex is preferred (most security budgets), rent. If your team has unused capex and wants to capitalize, purchase is available.

3. **Do you want to manage hardware lifecycle or have it managed for you?** Rental = managed. Purchase = your responsibility after support contract term.

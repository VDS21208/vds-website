## Why this comparison matters

Physical security guards are the default at most construction sites, logistics yards, and commercial properties because they're the default. Procurement understands the line item, the project manager has used the same guard company for ten years, and "we should add a guard" is the answer to most perimeter problems.

The math has changed. Wages have risen sharply since 2022. Insurance costs for guard companies have risen. Coverage gaps from sleeping-on-shift incidents are documented in court records. And the alternative — solar-autonomous surveillance with a 24/7 SOC running audio talk-down — is now cheap, deployable in hours, and produces video evidence that wins criminal cases and insurance claims.

This page is the math, the documented case study, and the honest answer about where guards still win.

## Where guards still win

**Physical intervention.** A trailer cannot tackle someone. If your specific risk profile requires physical intervention at the scene before law enforcement arrives, you need a human. We dispatch LE with live video in under 60 seconds — but a human guard already on-site is faster than a police response.

**Customer-facing service.** At a hotel, a country club, a high-end retail entrance, the uniformed presence is part of the brand. Guards do customer service work cameras can't.

**Specific access-control scenarios.** Manned gates with vehicle inspection, badge checks, package screening — these are guard tasks. VDS handles passive monitoring; guards handle active gatekeeping.

**Sites with persistent active threat.** Some sites — high-value cash handling, certain federal facilities, executive protection — require armed human presence. Our trailers don't replace that.

## Where VDS wins

**The cost math is brutal.** 24/7 guard coverage at a single site requires 3-4 FTEs (three 8-hour shifts plus overlap and PTO coverage). At a $20-$26/hr fully-loaded rate including benefits and overhead, that's $180,000-$440,000/year for one site. A Mobile Surveillance Unit covers the same perimeter at $26,400/year. The savings recover the cost of a security director's salary on a single property.

**Documented ROI on a real project.** On a $28M Mid-Atlantic general contractor project, we replaced a guard contract running $32,400/mo with a single trailer at $4,000/mo (all-in including SOC). Result: 73% theft reduction, $0 insurance claims for the project duration, $340,800 saved in 12 months. We'll share the project name under NDA on the architecture call.

**No sleeping-on-shift risk.** Guard industry turnover runs 100-300% annually. Sleeping-on-shift is documented in court records and insurance claim disputes industry-wide. Our SOC operates from a single Maryland facility with W-2 employees on tenure. Roll call is logged. Operator activity is logged. Footage of the operator's screen is auditable on request.

**Video evidence wins criminal and civil cases.** When something happens at a guarded site, what often exists is the guard's incident report — a paper document with a narrative. When something happens at a VDS site, what exists is time-stamped HD video of the event with a recorded audio talk-down by the operator and a logged LE dispatch. Prosecutors prefer video. Insurance adjusters prefer video.

**Audio talk-down deters before LE arrives.** The moment an analytic fires, our operator can speak through the trailer's amplified speaker: "You are being recorded by Vision Detection Systems Security Operations Center. Police have been dispatched. Leave the property immediately." Documented intrusion rate drops sharply at first contact. Guards do this in person; we do it remotely and consistently.

**Scales sub-linearly across sites.** A 10-site portfolio needs 10 trailers and the same SOC. A 10-site guarded portfolio needs 30-40 FTEs. The cost curve diverges hard above 3 sites.

## Why this comparison matters

WCCTV is a UK-origin mobile surveillance company that's marketed itself as "USA's #1 Supplier of Mobile Surveillance." They have a deep construction case study library — Turner Construction projects, LAX terminal expansions, IAH work — and a clean, simple rental model that's easy for a project manager to expense.

The gap is technological and operational. The platform is 2018-era. The AI story is light. There's no comparable in-house Security Operations Center. And the company's center of gravity is in the UK, which matters for data residency and procurement-side compliance work in 2026.

This page is for buyers who've seen WCCTV on a previous project and want to know what a US-headquartered, modern alternative looks like.

## Where WCCTV wins

**Construction case study depth.** Turner Construction, LAX, IAH — public references on jobs of meaningful scale. If your GC's procurement office wants a name with documented airport work, WCCTV has the references.

**Simple rental model.** No software licensing tier conversation, no SOC sub-pricing — pay the monthly, get the trailer. The simplicity is real. We match it, but we'll give them credit.

**Established US ops despite UK origin.** They've been operating in the US long enough to have real American customer support, US service trucks, and US install capacity. They are not a UK-only company shipping units across the Atlantic.

## Where VDS wins

**Modern AI stack.** WCCTV's analytics platform is 2018-era. Modern construction sites need perimeter analytics, loitering detection, tailgate detection, and plate-reading running concurrently with low false-positive rates. Our 2026 stack delivers 97% false-alarm reduction in production deployments.

**US assembly, US data, US SOC.** We assemble trailers in Pikesville, Maryland. Our cloud runs on US-only AWS regions. Our SOC operators are W-2 employees in the United States. For federal-adjacent construction, government work, or critical infrastructure projects, the all-American supply chain matters at the procurement compliance layer.

**24/7 in-house SOC included.** WCCTV expects the customer (or the customer's monitoring vendor) to handle the human-in-the-loop layer. VDS includes the SOC in every monthly. Audio talk-down with operator escalation, LE dispatch with live video, documented sub-60-second escalation — all under one contract.

**NDAA Section 889 by default.** Every VDS deployment is NDAA Section 889 compliant. WCCTV's reporting on this is mixed and you need to verify per-deployment. For federal-adjacent work, that's a real operational difference.

**Documented construction ROI.** On a $28M Mid-Atlantic general contractor project, VDS reduced theft 73%, eliminated insurance claims, and cut security cost from $32,400/mo to $4,000/mo — saving $340,800 in 12 months. We can provide the project name under NDA on the architecture call.

## Cost and TCO

WCCTV quotes are not published; reported customer monthlies fall roughly in the $1,800-$2,800/mo range depending on configuration. VDS publishes its rates: Sky Guard $2,200/mo, Swift Deploy $1,500/mo, Boundary Guard Pro $1,800/mo, all-in with SOC and VMS included.

Over a 12-month project, the difference is rarely the headline monthly — it's the variability. WCCTV monthlies on long projects often drift upward at refresh. Our pricing is locked for the term.

## Switching from WCCTV

1. **Calendar the existing rental.** WCCTV contracts are typically month-to-month after the initial 90-day term. Identify your cancellation window.
2. **Schedule the swap.** Our delivery team coordinates with the WCCTV pickup so there's no coverage gap. Typical swap is a single day for a single-trailer site.
3. **Re-mount fixed assets where applicable.** Site signage and access protocols transfer; SOC liaison is assigned before the swap.
4. **Document the construction phases.** Our team plans trailer redeployment as the site advances — different positions for foundation, frame, finish, and close-out phases.

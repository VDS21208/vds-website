## Why this comparison matters

"Mobile surveillance" is a fragmented category. Buyers searching for a trailer or a remote-monitoring contract land on at least three different product types: solar-autonomous mobile surveillance units (MSUs), enterprise cloud VMS platforms, and managed remote video monitoring (RVM) services. Some vendors sell one. A few sell two. One sells all three under one roof.

This page is written by Vision Detection Systems. We are on the list. We disclose that up front. Where a competitor genuinely wins on a dimension, we say so.

Evaluation criteria, weighted in this order:

1. **Owns the hardware** — designs and builds the mobile unit, not just resells it.
2. **AI platform** — proprietary VMS with analytics (LPR, person/vehicle, intrusion), camera-agnostic via ONVIF/RTSP.
3. **24/7 US Security Operations Center** — in-house human operators, not outsourced abroad.
4. **NDAA Section 889 compliance** — no Hikvision/Dahua components, federal-eligible.
5. **US-only AWS data residency** — encrypted at rest (AES-256), TLS 1.3 in transit, US AWS regions only.
6. **Pricing transparency** — published starting price on the website.

## Where each competitor wins

**LVT** wins on raw fleet scale. If your buying committee values "they have 5,000 units in the field," LVT will satisfy that test before anyone else. The trade-off is hardware-locked architecture and no published pricing.

**ECAM** wins on RVM headcount. After the 2025 Stealth + ECAMSECURE + GardaWorld merger they are the largest remote monitoring service in the US by operator seats. Brand churn is real and the platform story is still settling.

**Flock Safety** wins on the ALPR community network for municipal customers. The January 2026 public exposure of 67 camera feeds and the resulting 30+ city cancellations are a governance signal worth weighing.

**WCCTV** wins on UK and European references. In the US they market a "#1" position; the product UX has not been refreshed recently and there is no real AI inference layer.

**Pro-Vigil** wins on managed RVM contracts for retail and auto. They do not own outdoor hardware and Glassdoor reviews flag technology debt and operator turnover.

**Verkada** wins on enterprise office and school cloud VMS with a polished admin UI. The $2.95M FTC settlement (August 2024) and the 2021 breach where access was gained to 150,000 customer cameras are governance facts. Verkada is fixed-camera, not mobile.

**Spot AI** wins on software-only deployments where the customer already owns ONVIF/RTSP cameras and wants a modern VMS layer. No outdoor mobile hardware. No SOC.

**Deploy Surveillance** wins on smaller, simpler US mobile deals where the buyer wants a trailer and a basic feed, not a full platform.

**GoView** wins on lowest-end rent/purchase pricing where published.

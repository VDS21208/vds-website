## Why this comparison matters

Mobile surveillance units need power. Two architectures dominate the market: solar-plus-battery and diesel generator. Some vendors offer hybrid. VDS deploys solar-plus-battery as standard with a generator backup option for specific high-latitude winter sites.

The choice is consequential. Solar wins on operating cost, environmental compliance, theft profile, and noise. Diesel wins on uptime certainty in extreme low-light conditions and on continuous load capacity. This page lays out the math and the failure modes.

## The feature matrix

| Dimension | Diesel Generator | Solar + Battery (VDS standard) |
|---|---|---|
| Continuous runtime between intervention | 7-14 days | Indefinite (in spec sun) |
| Fuel cost per month | $400-$900 | $0 |
| Refuel logistics required | Yes — fuel truck or jerry cans | None |
| Maintenance cost / mo | $100-$300 (oil, filters, service) | <$20 amortized |
| Emissions (CO2, NOx, particulates) | Yes, regulated in some jurisdictions | None |
| Noise | 60-75 dB at unit | Silent |
| Fuel theft risk | Real (especially rural/remote sites) | None |
| Cold-weather start reliability | Variable (needs block heater below 20°F) | N/A |
| Performance in week-long overcast | Excellent | Designed for 7-10 day zero-sun |
| Performance in Arctic winter (4 hr daylight) | Excellent | Marginal — generator backup recommended |
| Carbon-footprint reporting | Counted | Zero |
| Restricted-area permitting | Often requires emissions permit | Typically permit-free |
| Footprint | Generator + fuel tank | Solar array + battery pack |
| Failure mode | Out of fuel = dark | Battery deep discharge = recovery cycle |
| Site-up time | Faster (no panel deploy) | 4-6 hours including panel deploy |

## Where diesel wins

**Arctic and sub-Arctic winter sites.** At latitudes above 60° N, December has 4 hours of daylight or less. Even oversized solar arrays struggle. Diesel runs regardless.

**Industrial sites with extreme continuous load.** If the surveillance trailer is also powering ancillary equipment (heated enclosure, supplemental lighting, secondary radar) the load may exceed solar generation capacity even with full sun. Diesel handles continuous heavy load.

**Sites with existing fuel logistics.** A construction site that already has a fuel truck on a weekly schedule for heavy equipment has effectively zero marginal cost to add a generator to the rotation. The fuel logistics cost is sunk.

**Continuous deployment over 7+ days of zero sun.** Where weather modeling shows sustained heavy overcast (Pacific Northwest winter, Great Lakes lake-effect snow corridors), an oversized solar array works but adds cost and footprint. Diesel with backup solar is sometimes more efficient.

## Where solar wins (and is VDS standard)

**Operating cost.** Solar fuel cost is zero. Battery replacement cost amortizes to <$20/mo. Diesel fuel runs $400-$900/mo plus $100-$300/mo service. Over a 24-month deployment, solar saves $12,000-$28,000 per unit.

**No refuel logistics.** Diesel needs a refuel cycle — a truck or human visiting the site every 7-14 days. That's an operating burden, a logistics cost, and a security exposure (the refuel team has access to your site weekly). Solar units run themselves.

**No emissions.** California air resources regulations, certain federal site requirements, and an increasing number of municipal permits restrict or prohibit diesel generator operation. Solar units sidestep the entire compliance conversation.

**No fuel theft target.** Rural and remote construction sites report diesel fuel theft as a recurring problem — a thief siphoning the generator's tank during overnight. Solar units have no fuel to steal. The panels and batteries are physically integrated into the trailer and difficult to remove without specific tools.

**Silent operation.** Diesel generators at 60-75 dB are audible at 100+ feet. On urban infill sites, that noise generates neighbor complaints and sometimes municipal noise-ordinance citations. Solar units are silent.

**Zero carbon footprint.** For customers reporting Scope 1/Scope 2 emissions to investors, regulators, or sustainability frameworks, solar surveillance is reported as zero. Diesel is counted.

**VDS designs for 7-10 day zero-sun autonomy.** Our battery sizing assumes a worst-case dark week. We deploy in Seattle, Portland, Chicago, Buffalo, and across the Northeast year-round without weather-driven shutdowns.

## Cost math over 24 months — single mid-load site

- **Diesel:** $600/mo fuel + $200/mo service = $800/mo × 24 = **$19,200 in operating cost above the unit rental**, plus the refuel-logistics labor (typically 2-4 hours per refuel event × ~80 events = $4,000-$8,000 labor).
- **Solar (VDS standard):** $0 fuel, <$20/mo amortized battery wear = **<$480 operating cost**, no logistics labor.

Net solar savings: **$22,720-$26,720 over 24 months per unit.**

## Failure-mode comparison

**Diesel failure modes:**
- Out of fuel — unit goes dark within hours of empty tank
- Cold-start failure below 20°F without block heater
- Mechanical failure (alternator, starter, fuel pump)
- Fuel theft or contamination
- Refuel-truck no-show or weather-delayed access

**Solar failure modes:**
- Battery deep-discharge after >10 days zero sun (recovery cycle on next sunny day)
- Panel obscuration (heavy snow, debris) — requires manual clear
- MPPT controller failure (rare, vendor-replaced)
- Battery wear at 8-10 years of cycling

The solar failure mode is recoverable. The diesel failure modes generally require a truck roll.

## When to specify generator backup

For sites at high latitude with sustained sub-2-hour daylight in winter, or sites with sustained heavy load above solar generation capacity, we'll specify a generator backup. The default unit is solar-only because the default site doesn't need diesel and benefits from operating-cost savings.

## How to decide

Three questions:

1. **Where is the site?** Below 50° N latitude with normal cloud cover: solar always wins. Above 60° N in winter: generator or hybrid.

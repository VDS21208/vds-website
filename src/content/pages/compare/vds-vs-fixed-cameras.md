## Why this comparison matters

Most security buyers default to "install fixed cameras" because that's how surveillance has been done for thirty years. Fixed cameras are the right answer for many use cases — a permanent office building, an interior production floor, a permanent loading dock. They are the wrong answer for any site where power, network, or geometry will change in the next 18 months.

The case for mobile solar surveillance comes down to three numbers: install lead time, total install cost including trenching and conduit, and redeployment cost when the site advances. This page lays out when each architecture is correct.

## Where fixed cameras win

**Permanent installations.** If the building isn't moving and the lot isn't changing for the next ten years, fixed cameras are the right architecture. They cost less per-camera at scale (after the install hump is paid).

**Density.** You can put 40 fixed cameras inside a warehouse for less per-camera than 40 mobile units. Where you need very high camera density on a permanent footprint, fixed wins.

**Indoor environment.** Inside a building with steady power and HVAC, fixed cameras live longer and cost less to maintain. We don't sell indoor solutions — that's Verkada, Spot AI, Avigilon territory.

**Specific architectural integration.** Where cameras need to integrate with existing access control, fire alarm, or building automation systems via wired protocols, fixed installs are often required.

## Where VDS wins

**Site-up in hours, not days.** A construction site that needs surveillance tomorrow waits 5-15 days for a fixed camera install — permit, trench, conduit, electrician, network drop, mount, configure. By then, the site has been hit twice. A VDS trailer arrives on a truck, levels in 90 minutes, and is recording within 4 hours.

**No trenching, no conduit, no electrician.** A typical fixed install on a vacant lot involves coordinating an electrician for power, a low-voltage contractor for network, and possibly a permit for trenching. Total cost commonly runs $3,000-$8,000 per camera before you ever see a frame of video. A VDS Sky Guard trailer needs an empty pad of dirt and a clear sky.

**Redeployable when the site changes.** Construction sites move. A camera positioned over the foundation phase is in the wrong place by the time framing starts. With fixed cameras, you pay an installer to relocate. With a VDS trailer, you hook a truck to the tongue and tow it to the new position. Cost: zero, time: an hour.

**Solar-autonomous off-grid.** Most construction sites don't have permanent power. Most parking fields don't have a 120V outlet at the back corner. Most water utility yards don't have network at the fence line. We bring our own power and our own network. Fixed cameras can't operate where there's no infrastructure.

**One contract for hardware + VMS + SOC.** Fixed cameras typically come with a camera invoice, an install invoice, a network configuration invoice, a VMS subscription, and (separately) a monitoring contract. VDS is one monthly that includes all of it.

**No capex.** Fixed cameras hit the capex budget — meaning they get argued over in budget cycles and depreciated over 5-7 years. VDS is opex, charged to the site's monthly security budget without procurement battles.

## Cost and TCO over 36 months — perimeter coverage at a 5-acre site

- **Fixed cameras:** 8 cameras × $2,800 installed = $22,400 capex. NVR + storage $4,000 capex. VMS license $80/cam/mo × 8 × 36 = $23,040. Monitoring contract $600/mo × 36 = $21,600. Power/network $200/mo × 36 = $7,200. **Total 36-month: $78,240.** Redeployment if site changes: $1,500-$4,000 per move.
- **VDS Sky Guard MSU:** $2,200/mo × 36 = **$79,200.** Redeployment included.

At 36 months the prices are similar. The difference is risk: if your site changes once during that period, the fixed install loses by $1,500-$4,000+. If you have 5-15 day site-up gaps, the fixed install loses by whatever was stolen in those gaps.

## When to pick which

**Pick fixed cameras when:**
- Permanent indoor enterprise deployment
- Building has reliable power and network at every camera location
- Site geometry will not change in the next 5+ years
- High camera density (40+ cameras inside a footprint)
- Capex budget is available and depreciation works
- You have a procurement window of 30+ days for install

**Pick VDS when:**
- Site needs surveillance within a week
- Site has no power or no network at the perimeter
- Site geometry will change inside the next 24 months
- You want opex, not capex
- You want SOC, VMS, and hardware on one contract
- You want to avoid trenching, conduit, and electrician coordination

**Pick both when:**
- Indoor enterprise + outdoor perimeter at the same property — common.

## Switching from a planned fixed install

1. **Re-scope the urgent need.** Most projects scoped for fixed install can run VDS at the perimeter during the install lead time, then decide whether the fixed install is still needed.
2. **Pilot the trailer.** A single Sky Guard deployment for 90 days proves out coverage and cost.
3. **Decide on permanent architecture.** Many customers find the trailer becomes the permanent answer for outdoor perimeter and the fixed install scope shrinks to just the building interior.

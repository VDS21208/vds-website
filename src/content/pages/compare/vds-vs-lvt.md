## Why this comparison matters

LiveView Technologies is the most-deployed mobile surveillance brand in the United States. If you've worked retail security for any major chain, you've seen their D3 unit. They built the category, and a lot of buyers default to "let's just call LVT" because of brand familiarity.

That default has gotten expensive. The LVT pricing model — D3 hardware at $38,000-$52,000 per unit plus a mandatory cloud subscription you cannot opt out of — locks customers into a closed ecosystem where the cameras, the analytics, and the monitoring are all single-vendor. If you ever want to add a third-party camera, run your own VMS, or move to a different monitoring center, the path is hard.

VDS sells a different shape. Hardware is rented monthly ($1,500-$2,200), the VMS accepts any ONVIF-compliant camera, and the SOC is an in-house operation in the United States — not outsourced. This page compares the two head-to-head.

## Where LVT wins

**Scale and brand recognition.** Roughly 5,000 units in the field is a real number. If you're a national retailer with a procurement team that wants the most-deployed option in writing, LVT clears that bar.

**Tier-one retail case study library.** Walmart, Home Depot, Lowe's, and Kroger are on record as LVT customers. If your CFO requires a named peer reference at exactly your tier, LVT has more of them.

**Vertical integration as a feature, not a bug.** Some buyers want one throat to choke. If you don't want to think about camera selection, VMS configuration, or SOC selection separately, LVT's closed model removes those decisions. That's a legitimate preference.

## Where VDS wins

**No hardware lock-in.** Our VMS is ONVIF-compliant and accepts cameras from Axis, Hanwha, Bosch, Hikvision (where compliance allows), Avigilon, and others. You can deploy our trailers alongside your existing fixed-camera estate without ripping anything out. LVT's platform only runs LVT cameras.

**Operating expense, not capital expense.** A $38K-$52K D3 unit is a CapEx line item that gets argued over in budget meetings. A $1,500-$2,200/mo VDS trailer is OpEx that hits the project's general conditions or the site's monthly security budget without a procurement battle. Over 12 months at a single site, the LVT unit costs roughly the same as 18-29 months of a VDS rental — but you own a depreciating asset you may not need next year.

**In-house SOC with sub-60-second escalation.** Our Security Operations Center is staffed in Pikesville, Maryland — not subcontracted. Operators see a live video pop-up the moment an analytic fires, run audio talk-down within 30 seconds, and dispatch local law enforcement with live video pushed to the responding officer's mobile. LVT routes monitoring through a third party, which adds handoff time.

**Documented ROI.** On a $28M Mid-Atlantic general contractor project, VDS reduced theft 73%, eliminated insurance claims, and cut security spend from $32,400/mo to $4,000/mo — saving $340,800 in 12 months. Receipt available under NDA.

## Cost and TCO over 36 months

For a single mid-complexity site running 24/7:

- **LVT D3:** $45,000 average hardware + $1,200/mo subscription estimate = $45,000 + $43,200 over 36 months = **$88,200**, plus you own a unit that's now 3 years into its depreciation curve.
- **VDS Sky Guard MSU:** $2,200/mo all-in = **$79,200** over 36 months, no terminal hardware liability, no resale obligation.

At month 37, the LVT customer is sitting on hardware that needs maintenance contracts and eventual replacement. The VDS customer either cancels, renews, or upgrades to the latest hardware generation at no incremental cost.

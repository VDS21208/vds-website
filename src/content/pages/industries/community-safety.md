## The challenge in community safety

HOA boards and property managers carry liability for incidents that occur on association-owned property — pool decks, clubhouses, mail kiosks, visitor parking, construction staging during a roof or paving project. Hardwired cameras require trenching board approval, capital reserves, and an IT contract nobody wants to manage. Off-duty patrols cost $35–55 per hour and produce no video evidence after the fact.

VDS operates a private-property security model. Cameras are sited only on land your association controls, with sightlines reviewed before deployment to keep neighboring lots, sidewalks, and public right-of-way out of frame. This is not a community camera-sharing network and not a Flock-style license-plate dragnet — it is private surveillance of private amenities, owned by the association, monitored on the association's behalf.

## How VDS protects community sites

Each deployment starts with a sightline walk: where the unit goes, what it sees, and what gets masked off. AI VisionStream applies privacy zones to neighboring property, the public street, and any window-bearing residence in view. Cameras are camera-agnostic over ONVIF/RTSP, so a board that already owns clubhouse PTZ cameras can route those feeds into the same VMS without ripping anything out.

When motion crosses an active zone after hours, the US-based SOC verifies on live video within 60 seconds. Verified events trigger a two-way audio warn-off, a recorded incident packet, and dispatch to local PD with a video link the responding officer can open from the cruiser. False-alarm rate runs around 3% across deployed sites.

Typical community use cases:
- **Amenity-area monitoring** — pool decks after hours, fitness rooms, dog parks, mail/package kiosks
- **Visitor and overflow parking** — vehicle break-ins, abandoned vehicles, unauthorized commercial parking
- **Construction-stage coverage** — re-roofs, paving, clubhouse renovations where contractors stage materials on common ground
- **Post-storm temporary coverage** — hurricane, ice storm, or tornado response when power and existing cameras are down for weeks

## The challenge: assets without power, theft without witnesses

The Permian, the Bakken, the Marcellus — the geography that makes the field economic is the same geography that makes surveillance hard. A typical wellsite is 5-20 miles from a paved road, 20-40 miles from a sheriff's deputy, and has no commercial power within a reasonable run. Pipeline ROWs are hundreds of miles long. Compressor stations are unmanned overnight.

Copper theft alone costs the energy sector hundreds of millions annually — the FBI's 2024 Insurance Information Institute summary tracked rising claims across producing basins. Add equipment theft, vandalism, and the regulatory exposure of an unauthorized person standing next to a wellhead, and the case for monitored surveillance is straightforward — but the wired approach is impossible.

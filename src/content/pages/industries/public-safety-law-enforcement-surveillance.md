## The Public Safety & Law Enforcement Security Problem

<p>Law enforcement agencies, municipal governments, and public safety departments need surveillance capabilities deployable rapidly without weeks-long procurement and installation cycles. Elevated crime areas, special events, emergency response situations, and public infrastructure protection require a surveillance solution that can be operational in hours, not months.</p>

<p>Federal procurement requirements — particularly NDAA Section 889 compliance — add a layer of complexity that eliminates most commercial surveillance vendors from consideration. Documentation requirements, audit logging, and evidence chain-of-custody standards eliminate off-the-shelf consumer and prosumer camera systems entirely. According to the <a href="https://www.fbi.gov/how-we-can-help-you/safety-resources/scams-and-safety/common-scams-and-crimes/violent-crime" target="_blank" rel="nofollow">FBI's Uniform Crime Reporting Program</a>, violent crime rates in targeted deployment areas can fluctuate rapidly, requiring surveillance solutions that match this operational pace.</p>

<p>Traditional security approaches fall short for public safety operations. Permanent CCTV installations require 2–6 weeks for permitting, trenching, and installation — too slow for emerging crime patterns or emergency response. Security guards cost $18,000–$36,000 per month for 24/7 coverage and provide limited documentation capabilities. Basic camera systems lack the AI detection, verified response protocols, and evidence formatting that law enforcement requires for successful prosecution.</p>

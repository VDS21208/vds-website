This page exists because every procurement conversation eventually lands on this question. We'll give you the math, the qualitative factors, and a clear decision tree.

## The headline math

At the Mobile Surveillance Unit rate ($2,200/mo rental, $38,000 purchase):

| Month | Cumulative Rental Cost | Purchase + Platform/SOC |
|---|---|---|
| 12 | $26,400 | $50,000 ($38K + $12K opex) |
| 18 | $39,600 | $56,000 |
| 24 | $52,800 | $62,000 |
| **30 (break-even)** | **$66,000** | **$68,000** |
| 36 | $79,200 | $74,000 |
| 48 | $105,600 | $86,000 |

Break-even hits around month 30 once you include $1,000/mo of platform + SOC opex that applies to both scenarios. Without SOC and platform costs (just the trailer hardware), break-even moves to month 17.

The pure-hardware view is misleading because almost no one buys a trailer without monitoring. Use the 30-month figure for honest decision-making.

## When to rent

Rent if any of these is true:

- **Project duration is under 24 months** — most construction projects, event security, disaster response, retail seasonal.
- **You need to redeploy** — multi-site portfolios where the trailer follows the active project. Rental terms include redeployment between sites for $500-$1,200 per move.
- **Capex is constrained** — budgets that allocate to opex are easier to approve and don't require depreciation tracking or asset-management overhead.
- **You want bundled service** — deployment, pickup, maintenance, platform, and (optionally) SOC all in one monthly invoice.
- **Single-site duration is uncertain** — if the site might wrap in 8 months or 28 months, rental protects against committing $38K to a uncertain horizon.

## When to buy

Buy if all three are true:

- **Site duration is 36+ months at a fixed location** — long-term industrial sites, permanent commercial parking lots, established utility yards.
- **Capex is available and preferred** — your finance team prefers capitalized assets, depreciation runway, or property-tax-deductible classification.
- **Maintenance overhead is acceptable** — you have facilities staff who can swap a camera or clean a solar panel without a service call.

## The 24-36 month decision

This is the window where the math is close enough that other factors decide it. Use this checklist:

| Question | If yes... | If no... |
|---|---|---|
| Will the trailer move between sites? | Rent | Lean buy |
| Is capex available without offset trade-offs? | Lean buy | Rent |
| Will you replace cameras or hardware within 3 years? | Rent (avoid asset write-down) | Either |
| Is your depreciation period 5+ years? | Buy (longer recovery) | Rent |
| Do you want predictable monthly opex on the security line? | Rent | Buy |
| Is this a one-time project? | Rent | Buy |

## Hidden costs of ownership

Buyers underestimate four things:

1. **Maintenance** — extended warranty is $1,800/year for years 2+. Without it, expect $500-$2,000/year in repair costs.
2. **Storage** — when not deployed, the trailer needs a yard. Some customers absorb this; others pay $100-$300/mo for outdoor storage.
3. **Resale value** — surveillance trailers depreciate 15-25% per year. A $38K trailer is worth roughly $20K-$25K at year 3 and $12K-$18K at year 5.
4. **Tech obsolescence** — cameras, AI models, and cellular standards (4G LTE Sunset is approaching) all evolve. A trailer purchased today is 2-3 years from a meaningful refresh need.

VDS purchases include 1-year warranty standard. Extended warranty ($1,800/yr) covers parts, labor, and cellular module upgrades.

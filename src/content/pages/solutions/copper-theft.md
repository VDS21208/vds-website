## The construction copper theft problem in 2026

Copper has run between $4.20 and $4.85 per pound on the LME spot market through Q1 2026. A typical 300-unit multifamily site stages roughly 18,000 to 26,000 pounds of copper wire, plumbing, and HVAC line set over the rough-in phase. That is $75,000 to $125,000 of scrap value sitting on a job site overnight, with no occupants, no lighting after 7pm, and a fence that a bolt cutter defeats in under 4 seconds.

Thieves work in three patterns:
- **Pre-dawn cut crews (3am-5am)** — 2-3 person team, white work van, hi-vis vests, they look like an early shift. They strip energized service drops, panel feeders, and stacked spools.
- **Insider tips** — a former laborer, an unbadged subcontractor, or a delivery driver who saw the spool count. 38% of jobsite theft involves prior site knowledge per IRMI loss data.
- **Weekend sweeps** — Saturday afternoon through Sunday night, when general contractors and most subs are off-site for 60+ hours.

Standard reactive CCTV records the theft. It does not stop it. Recovery is below 7% (FBI UCR).

VDS treats the laydown yard as a defended perimeter, not a recorded space. The detection-to-response chain runs in under 60 seconds:

2. **Radar correlation** — radar confirms a moving target on the same vector. 97% false alarm reduction versus motion-only systems.
3. **SOC operator verification** — a US-based operator pulls 4K visual, confirms the intrusion, and triggers the on-unit speaker.
4. **Audio talk-down** — "You are on private property under live surveillance. Local law enforcement has been dispatched. Leave the site immediately." Roughly 9 out of 10 intrusions end here.
5. **LE dispatch with live video** — if the subject does not leave, the SOC pushes a live feed to dispatch and stays on the line until arrival.

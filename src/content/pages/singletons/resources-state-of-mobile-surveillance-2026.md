## What's in the 2026 report

The State of Mobile Surveillance is VDS's annual industry analysis. It's free, it's cited, and it's intentionally structured for the audience that actually procures and operates these systems — security directors, construction risk managers, federal procurement officers, insurance carrier risk engineering teams, and the journalists covering the space.

### 1. 2026 mobile surveillance market sizing
North American mobile surveillance market estimate by segment — construction, utilities, government and municipal, retail and commercial, critical infrastructure. Methodology disclosed: which third-party market research firms' baselines we triangulated, what assumptions we adjusted, where we disagree with consensus and why.

### 2. Organized retail crime trends (NRF data + VDS observations)
2025 NRF National Retail Security Survey figures on retail crime losses, organized retail crime (ORC) incidents, and the security technology adoption patterns retailers are actually executing. VDS field observations from 80+ retail deployment sites across 14 states overlay where our data agrees with — and contradicts — the NRF national picture.

### 3. Construction theft and vandalism economics (FBI UCR + NICB data)
FBI Uniform Crime Reporting and NICB data on construction site theft, vandalism, equipment theft, and copper-and-wire theft trends. Per-incident loss distribution. Geographic concentration. What's actually being stolen versus what construction professionals assume is being stolen — the gap is informative.

### 4. Mobile surveillance versus legacy alternatives — full cost and performance comparison
Line-by-line comparison of mobile surveillance against legacy security alternatives:
- Physical guard services ($35-65/hour, single-point coverage)
- Fixed CCTV with no monitoring (deterrent claim, no response)
- Owner self-monitoring (alert fatigue, inconsistent response)
- Hybrid guard + camera programs

Methodology and assumptions documented. Worked-example math for a hypothetical 4-site portfolio over 24 months.

### 5. VDS field observations from 500+ deployments
Operational data from the VDS deployment base across 30+ states:
- Median alert volume by vertical (construction, utility, retail, dealership)
- False alarm rate trend through 2025 (down to 3% — the 97% reduction figure)
- Talk-down event count and outcome distribution
- LE dispatch frequency by site type
- Most common detected incident types by quarter

All anonymized — no customer is individually identifiable. Aggregate data with methodology disclosure.

### 6. NDAA Section 889 and the camera supply chain
A look at how the regulatory environment around foreign-manufactured cameras has evolved through 2024-2026, what's now compliant versus not, and the practical implications for security buyers operating in federal-adjacent, defense, and grant-funded environments. Includes the state-level patchwork (Florida, Texas, Georgia, others).

### 7. AI in physical security — where the hype ends and the operational reality begins
A grounded view of what AI is actually doing in physical security in 2026 — license plate recognition accuracy benchmarks, behavior analytics false positive rates, the limits of facial recognition (and why some vendors deliberately don't deploy it), generative AI's role and non-role in alert triage.

### 8. 2027 predictions
Six specific, falsifiable predictions for 2027:
- Where the market will grow fastest
- Which regulatory shifts to plan for
- What the hardware roadmap looks like across the industry
- Where insurance and surveillance economics will converge
- Two predictions about what won't happen despite vendor hype

Predictions are testable. We'll grade them publicly in the 2027 report.

## Why this report exists

Mobile surveillance is a category that has been undersized in mainstream security industry research for the better part of a decade. Pro-Vigil's annual report is the most-cited reference precisely because nobody else publishes the operational data with methodology disclosed.

VDS publishes the State of Mobile Surveillance to do the same job for our part of the market — and to put on the record what 500+ deployments actually look like, not what marketing decks claim.

## Methodology and citation

This report cites every operational claim to a source: peer-reviewed research where available, government data (FBI UCR, NIBRS, NICB) for crime statistics, third-party market research (referenced by firm and report) for sizing, and VDS field data for our own observations with sample sizes disclosed.

The full methodology section in the report covers data collection, aggregation, anonymization, and the limits of what we are claiming. Journalists and researchers are encouraged to cite specific findings with attribution to the report.

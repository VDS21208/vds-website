## Customer profile

A regional electric distribution utility operating in the Mid-Atlantic with approximately 240,000 metered customers. The customer requested anonymization for security and regulatory reasons. The three substations covered in this case study are distribution-level (not transmission), located in suburban-to-exurban areas with limited natural surveillance and adjacent right-of-way access.

## The problem

Across a six-month window, the utility experienced six confirmed copper theft incidents at three substations. The pattern was consistent: late-night perimeter breach through fence-cutting, theft of grounding conductor, ground-grid jumpers, and in one incident the copper bushings off a de-energized transformer staged for replacement. The direct material replacement cost per incident averaged $14,000 to $38,000, but the larger operational exposure was the safety risk to first responders and the regulatory scrutiny from the state PUC tied to repeated perimeter compromise events.

Total measured exposure across the six-month window: approximately $138,000 in replaced copper, $26,000 in emergency repair labor, $18,000 in LE callout and incident-response fees (the township billed for repeated after-hours calls), and an unquantified but material regulatory liability under NERC CIP-014 reporting thresholds for physical security incidents at distribution facilities adjacent to load-pocket transmission.

The utility evaluated three options: install fixed IP camera systems with on-site recording (estimated $145,000 per substation, 14-week procurement and install cycle, no live monitoring), contract local guard services ($186,000/year per substation for overnight coverage), or deploy mobile surveillance with 24/7 monitoring.

## The configuration

VDS deployed one Sky Guard MSU at each of the three substations. Sky Guard was selected over Swift Deploy because the substations required permanent multi-year coverage rather than redeployable mobility, and because the unit's extended-runtime solar profile fit the utility's preference for zero grid dependency on the surveillance system itself. Each unit was positioned at the corner of the substation fence line with the longest sight lines to the fence perimeter and the staged equipment areas.

All units fed into the VDS Cloud VMS. The utility's own physical security operations center maintained a viewer credential for situational awareness, but live monitoring and dispatch coordination ran through the VDS SOC. Audio talk-down was enabled at every unit. Two-stage escalation was configured: SOC operator verification within 30 seconds, audio talk-down within 60 seconds for verified intrusions, simultaneous LE dispatch and utility security duty officer notification for any verified perimeter breach.

NDAA Section 889 compliance was a procurement requirement. All hardware deployed met that standard.

## The first 60 days

Within the first three weeks, the SOC issued 22 audio talk-downs across the three sites. Nine of those involved subjects actively scaling or cutting the fence. Seven resulted in immediate departure. Two escalated to LE dispatch with arrests. The utility's security duty officer received four after-hours notifications, all confirmed as legitimate threats rather than false alarms — a sharp contrast with the prior fixed-alarm system that had generated an average of 2.4 false alarms per night across the three sites.

By day 60, the SOC was logging fewer than three approach events per week across the entire three-site portfolio.

## The 9-month outcome

Across the nine-month measurement window:

- **Confirmed copper theft incidents:** Zero (down from six in the prior comparable window).
- **Material protected (estimated value at risk):** $180,000 — calculated from the average per-incident loss in the pre-deployment window multiplied by the expected incident rate.
- **LE callout fees avoided:** $42,000 — the township's billed rate for after-hours response calls. The utility shifted from an average of 1.1 LE callouts per month to 0.3.
- **False alarm rate:** Under 2% of all SOC-reviewed events.
- **Audio talk-downs:** 58 across the portfolio.
- **LE dispatches with arrests:** 4.
- **System downtime:** Zero. All three units sustained operation through two named winter storms and a three-day regional grid outage that affected the substations themselves — the surveillance kept running on solar and battery while the substations were de-energized.

## What the customer said

The utility's physical security manager noted in the renewal review: "The substations stayed dark for 72 hours and the cameras did not. That is the headline."

## Customer profile

A general contracting firm headquartered in the Mid-Atlantic, $28M in annual revenue, running three concurrent commercial construction projects ranging from a mixed-use mid-rise to a regional distribution center. Combined active jobsite footprint: approximately 14 acres. The firm self-performs concrete, structural steel coordination, and MEP rough-in oversight.

## The problem

Across the prior 18 months, the GC absorbed an average of $42,500 per month in direct theft loss: copper wire pulls from staged conduit runs, three stolen 5500-watt portable generators, two skid steers (one recovered, one written off), small tools and fuel from gang boxes, and a recurring pattern of late-night perimeter breaches that triggered insurance scrutiny. The firm's commercial insurance carrier flagged the loss ratio and signaled that the next renewal would either include a theft exclusion or a premium increase the principal estimated at $60,000 to $90,000 annually.

The CFO and the firm's risk manager evaluated three alternatives: hire two overnight guards per active site (estimated $312,000/year per site for two 12-hour posts), install trenched conduit and pole-mounted IP cameras at each site (estimated $85,000 capex per site, six-week deployment, abandoned at project close), or deploy mobile surveillance with live monitoring.

## The configuration

VDS deployed two Swift Deploy mobile surveillance trailers and three Solar Camera Poles across the three active sites. Deployment summary:

- **Site A (mid-rise, 4.5 acres):** One Swift Deploy at the primary site entrance covering the material lay-down yard. Two Solar Camera Poles at the rear perimeter where the access road met an adjacent commercial property.
- **Site B (distribution center pad, 6.2 acres):** One Swift Deploy at the southwest corner covering the generator and fuel storage area. One Solar Camera Pole at the northeast corner covering staged copper and electrical materials.
- **Site C (interior renovation, 3.1 acres):** Coverage shared with Site B via repositioning between phases; no permanent unit on this site after week 6.

All units fed into the VDS Cloud VMS, with 24/7 monitoring by the US-based SOC. Audio talk-down enabled at every unit. Local LE coordination established with the township police departments serving each site in week one.

## The first 30 days

Within the first ten days, the SOC issued 14 audio talk-downs. Eleven resulted in immediate departure of the subject. Three escalated to LE dispatch — average response time 11 minutes, two arrests on Site A for trespass and attempted larceny. By day 30, the foot-traffic pattern at all three sites had measurably shifted: the SOC was logging an average of 0.8 perimeter approaches per night across the portfolio, down from a pre-deployment baseline the firm's own night-shift superintendent estimated at 4 to 6.

## The 12-month outcome

Across the measurement year:

- **Theft incidents:** 8 (down from a baseline of 29 in the prior comparable window) — a 73% reduction.
- **Direct theft loss:** $14,200 (down from a baseline of approximately $355,000) — $340,800 in avoided loss.
- **Insurance claims filed:** Zero. The renewal came back with the prior premium and no exclusion.
- **SOC audio talk-downs:** 187 across the portfolio.
- **LE dispatches:** 9. Five resulted in arrests, four were clear-on-arrival.
- **False alarm rate:** Under 3% of all SOC-reviewed events, consistent with the platform's documented 97% false alarm reduction.
- **Downtime:** Zero. Solar-autonomous units sustained continuous operation through two ice events and one named tropical storm.

## What the customer said

The firm's risk manager summarized the deployment in a single line during the contract renewal call: "We spent under $90,000 to avoid $340,000 in loss and an insurance fight we were going to lose. The math is not complicated."

## What we would do differently

In retrospect, Site C did not require its own unit. The repositioning model we adopted in week six produced equivalent coverage and reduced the customer's monthly spend. We now recommend this approach by default on portfolios with three or more small concurrent sites and adjacent access.

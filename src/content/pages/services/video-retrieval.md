Pulling video for evidence is a process, not a button. The wrong workflow — wrong timestamp, missing hash, ambiguous custodian — turns useful footage into something a defense attorney can challenge or an insurer can reject. The VDS SOC handles retrieval as a managed service so the chain stays intact.

## Two paths in

**Customer-initiated retrieval** — a manager, loss prevention officer, or risk team member opens a ticket through the portal, email, or phone. We need: site, approximate time window, what happened or what is being looked for. Response: median 30 minutes for standard requests during business hours, 90 minutes overnight.

**Law-enforcement-initiated retrieval** — an investigating officer calls our SOC line, provides agency, case number, and warrant or subpoena if applicable. We confirm the customer's release authorization policy on file, pull the clip, and deliver direct to the agency portal or evidence intake system. The customer is copied on the audit trail.

For active emergencies (in-progress incident, exigent circumstances), the SOC can authorize same-call release under the customer's pre-signed law-enforcement authorization. Most law-enforcement customers and many commercial customers maintain a standing release; the form is one page.

## What "chain of custody" actually means here

Every export carries a chain-of-custody manifest:

- Source camera ID and site ID
- Source timestamp range (UTC and local)
- SOC operator who generated the export
- Generation timestamp
- SHA-256 hash of the exported file
- AWS region of origin
- Audit log reference for the underlying source recording
- Recipient (customer user, law enforcement agency, insurer)
- Delivery method and timestamp

The manifest is a signed PDF generated by the platform and is reproducible on demand from the immutable 12-month audit log. If anyone asks "are you sure this is the original clip" — yes, and here is the hash to prove it.

## Delivery

Three options, customer chooses at tenant provisioning:

- **Signed portal link** — recipient downloads through HTTPS with TLS 1.3, link expires in 72 hours by default, single-recipient
- **Direct evidence-system integration** — Axon Evidence.com, NICE Investigate, Genetec ClearID, Motorola CommandCentral; we push the file with manifest attached
- **Physical media** — encrypted USB shipped by courier, used rarely for very large exports or air-gapped agencies

All deliveries are logged. The original source video is not deleted; the export is a copy, the source remains in retention until its retention policy expires.

## Common request types

- Workplace incident investigations (HR, EHS)
- Theft, shrinkage, internal loss prevention
- Slip-and-fall and other premises liability
- Auto incidents at logistics yards
- Construction safety incidents and OSHA recordable review
- Vandalism, trespassing, criminal mischief
- Law-enforcement support for outside-property crime where our cameras captured a vehicle or person of interest

## What the SOC operator actually does

1. Validate the requester against the authorization matrix
2. Identify the correct camera(s) by site and time
3. Frame the export window with appropriate pre-roll (default 60s) and post-roll
4. Preview the clip — confirms the right incident is captured, flags any privacy-sensitive content for redaction
5. Generate the export with manifest
6. Deliver via the chosen channel
7. Log every step to the audit trail

Median operator handle time on a clean request: 12 minutes. Median end-to-end customer turnaround: under 60 minutes.

## Privacy and redaction

For requests crossing jurisdictions with privacy obligations (HIPAA-adjacent healthcare, GDPR for international subsidiaries, state biometric laws), the SOC can request a redaction pass — face blur, license plate blur, or audio mute — before delivery. Redactions are reversible internally for court-ordered un-redaction but the delivered file is the redacted version with a separate hash.
